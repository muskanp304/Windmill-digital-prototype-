Wind Turbine Subsystems & Energy Flow

Core electro-mechanical stages involved in converting atmospheric kinetic energy to clean electrical grid power.

Wind Energy

Moving air masses possess kinetic energy proportional to air density and the cube of wind velocity (v³). The turbine rotor intercepts this kinetic energy stream.

Ek = ½ m v²

Rotor & Shaft

Aerodynamic lift forces on three aerofoil blades produce torque, turning the central rotor hub and low-speed shaft into mechanical rotational energy.

RPM = Wind Speed × 6

Generator

Permanent magnet induction or synchronous generator converts mechanical shaft torque into an alternating electrical electromagnetic induction field.

P = ½ ρ A Cp v³

Electrical Output

Power electronics condition raw voltage and current for consumer load delivery. Voltage scales proportionally with rotor RPM.

V = RPM × 0.5 • I = P / V

Battery Storage

Surplus energy generated beyond consumer demand charges chemical storage cells. When wind drops, the battery smoothly discharges to maintain load stability.

Psurplus = Pgen - Pload

STUDENT GUIDE

How a Wind Turbine Works

A simple, student-friendly engineering breakdown of the electro-mechanical principles.

01

Wind Energy

Moving air possesses mass and velocity. As it flows across the turbine blades, the aerodynamic shape creates a pressure differential (lift and drag).

02

Rotor Rotation

The aerodynamic lift forces overcome mechanical drag, causing the three turbine blades to rotate around the central aerodynamic rotor hub.

03

Shaft Rotation

The rotating rotor turns a main drive shaft housed within the nacelle, transferring mechanical torque directly into the drivetrain.

04

Generator

The generator uses magnetic induction to convert the mechanical rotation of the shaft into electrical current (Faraday's Law of Induction).

05

Electrical Output

The generated electrical voltage and current are regulated and fed directly to meet connected electrical appliances or consumer load demand.

06

Battery Storage

When wind power exceeds consumer load demand, surplus electricity charges the battery. If wind slows, the battery discharges to keep power uninterrupted.

MATHEMATICAL MODEL

Governing Simulation Equations

The physics formulas driving the real-time calculations in this simulation engine.

1. Aerodynamic Power Equation
Power = 0.5 × ρ × A × Cp × v³
• ρ (rho): Air density = 1.225 kg/m³
• A: Rotor swept area = 1.0 m²
• Cp: Power performance coefficient = 0.35 (Betz limit fraction)
• v: Wind speed in meters per second (m/s)
2. Rotor RPM Calculation
RPM = Wind Speed × 6
• Simplified educational relationship between wind velocity and rotational speed.
• Example: 10 m/s → 60 RPM • 20 m/s → 120 RPM • 30 m/s → 180 RPM
• At 0 m/s wind speed, RPM drops to zero.
3. Terminal Voltage
Voltage = RPM × 0.5
• Direct linear proportional relationship between generator shaft RPM and generated electromotive force (EMF).
• At 60 RPM: Voltage = 30.0 V • At 0 RPM: Voltage = 0.0 V
4. Output Current & Load Power
Current = Power / Voltage
Load Power = Generated Power × Load %
• Derived using standard electrical power formula (P = V × I).
• Zero-voltage safety handled cleanly (no NaN or Infinity when stopped).
• Surplus Power = Generated Power − Load Power.
ℹ️
Engineering Educational Disclaimer:

“These equations are simplified educational simulation models and are not a full real-world wind turbine model.”

Real commercial megawatt turbines incorporate active blade pitch angle control, variable-speed doubly-fed induction generators (DFIG), complex gearbox ratios, yaw drives, and turbulent cut-out limits.
